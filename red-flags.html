⚠️ Emergency Warning Signs

Red Flag Symptoms That Need Immediate Attention

Certain symptoms are medical emergencies or indicate serious conditions that need urgent evaluation. Learn to recognize these warning signs.

🚨 Call 911 Immediately

For life-threatening emergencies. Don't drive yourself — emergency responders can begin treatment en route.

Life-Threatening Emergencies

These symptoms require immediate emergency care. Call 911 or go directly to the nearest emergency room.

Call 911 For:

  • Chest pain or pressure — especially if it spreads to arm, jaw, or back, or comes with sweating, nausea, or shortness of breath
  • Difficulty breathing — severe shortness of breath, gasping, or feeling like you're suffocating
  • Signs of stroke — sudden face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty (use FAST below)
  • Severe allergic reaction — throat swelling, difficulty breathing, widespread hives, after exposure to allergen
  • Severe bleeding — uncontrolled bleeding that won't stop with direct pressure
  • Loss of consciousness — fainting, unresponsiveness, altered mental status
  • Seizures — especially first-time seizure or seizure lasting more than 5 minutes
  • Suicidal thoughts or self-harm — call 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or 911

Recognizing a Stroke: BE FAST

Stroke requires immediate treatment. Every minute matters — brain cells die rapidly without blood flow. Use this mnemonic to recognize stroke symptoms:

🧠 BE FAST for Stroke Recognition

BBalance — Sudden loss of balance or coordination

EEyes — Sudden vision changes in one or both eyes

FFace — Ask them to smile. Does one side droop?

AArms — Ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift downward?

SSpeech — Ask them to repeat a simple phrase. Is speech slurred or strange?

TTime — If you see ANY of these signs, call 911 immediately!

Symptoms That Always Need Medical Evaluation

These "red flag" symptoms may not require 911, but warrant prompt evaluation by a healthcare provider — don't ignore them.

🩸 Blood Where It Shouldn't Be

  • Coughing up blood
  • Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds
  • Blood in urine (pink, red, or cola-colored)
  • Blood in stool or black, tarry stools
  • Unexplained bruising or bleeding

🌡️ Fever Warning Signs

  • Fever over 103°F (39.4°C) in adults
  • Fever lasting more than 3 days
  • Fever with severe headache and stiff neck (meningitis warning)
  • Fever with rash that doesn't blanch when pressed
  • Fever in immunocompromised individuals
  • Any fever in infants under 3 months

⚡ Sudden or Severe Onset

  • "Worst headache of my life" — sudden, severe
  • Sudden vision loss or double vision
  • Sudden severe abdominal pain
  • Sudden weakness or numbness
  • Sudden confusion or difficulty speaking
  • Symptoms that rapidly worsen over hours

📉 Unexplained Changes

  • Unintentional weight loss (10+ lbs over 6-12 months)
  • Night sweats (soaking bedsheets)
  • Persistent fatigue not explained by lifestyle
  • New lumps or masses that persist
  • Changes in bowel or bladder habits lasting 2+ weeks
  • Persistent pain that won't go away

😴 Symptoms That Wake You

  • Pain that wakes you from sleep
  • Shortness of breath when lying flat
  • Waking up gasping for air
  • Night pain in back or joints (especially if persistent)

Special Populations

Children & Infants

Red flags that warrant immediate care in children:

  • Any fever in infants under 3 months old
  • Difficulty breathing or rapid breathing
  • Blue or gray skin color
  • Not waking up or not responding normally
  • Refusing to eat or drink for extended periods
  • Severe vomiting or diarrhea with signs of dehydration
  • Rash that doesn't fade when pressed (petechiae)
  • Bulging fontanelle (soft spot) in infants

Elderly Adults (65+)

Older adults may present differently — watch for:

  • Sudden confusion or changes in mental status
  • Falls or sudden inability to walk
  • Infections without typical fever
  • Heart attack without classic chest pain
  • Dehydration progressing rapidly

Pregnant Women

Seek immediate care for:

  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Severe headache with vision changes (preeclampsia warning)
  • Sudden swelling of face, hands, or feet
  • Decreased fetal movement
  • Fluid leaking from vagina before 37 weeks

When in Doubt

If you're unsure whether your symptoms are serious:

  • Trust your instincts — if something feels seriously wrong, it might be
  • Call your doctor's office — many have nurse advice lines available 24/7
  • Use telehealth — a quick video visit can help determine if you need emergency care
  • Go to urgent care — for concerning but non-life-threatening symptoms
  • Call 911 if in doubt — paramedics would rather evaluate you than have you wait too long

Better to seek care and learn it wasn't serious than to wait and have a poor outcome.
