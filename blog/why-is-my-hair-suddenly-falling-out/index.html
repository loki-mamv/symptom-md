Why Is My Hair Suddenly Falling Out?

October 13, 2026 7 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida
Table of Contents

  2. What causes sudden hair shedding all over the scalp?

The short answer: Sudden shedding across the whole scalp is most often telogen effluvium, a temporary reaction to a stress on the body. The trigger is usually an illness, surgery, childbirth, weight loss, or major stress from a few months earlier. The American Academy of Dermatology says hair tends to regain its normal fullness within six to nine months. See a clinician if hair falls out in patches, the scalp is red, scaly, or painful, or you have other new symptoms.

How much hair loss is normal?

Some daily shedding is healthy. The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) says it is normal to shed between 50 and 100 hairs a day. MedlinePlus gives a similar figure: losing up to 100 scalp hairs a day is normal, and most people regrow them.

The AAD separates two problems that look alike:

  • Excessive hair shedding. The body sheds many more hairs than normal each day. The medical name is telogen effluvium. It often stops on its own.
  • Hair loss. Something stops the hair from growing. The hair does not regrow until the cause stops.

You can have both at the same time. A clinician can tell them apart.

What causes sudden hair shedding all over the scalp?

A review in the Journal of Clinical and Diagnostic Research calls telogen effluvium a most common cause of diffuse hair loss. Diffuse means spread across the scalp, not in one spot. The AAD lists these common stressors:

  • Losing 20 pounds or more
  • Giving birth
  • Major stress, such as a divorce, a job loss, or caring for a sick relative
  • A high fever, or recovery from an illness
  • An operation
  • Stopping birth control pills

MedlinePlus says physical or emotional stress may cause one half to three quarters of scalp hair to shed. Hair tends to come out in handfuls when you shampoo, comb, or run your hands through it. That is alarming, but the follicles are not destroyed.

COVID-19 is a known trigger. A systematic review in the Journal of Medical Virology pooled 19 studies with 465 patients who had this shedding after COVID-19. Most of them recovered from the hair loss. A few had shedding that continued.

Unplanned weight loss needs its own check. Our guide on unintentional weight loss as a warning sign explains when to act.

How long after a trigger does hair fall out?

The delay is the part that confuses most people. The shedding does not start on the day of the stress. The review above reports that diffuse shedding is seen 3 to 4 months after the triggering event. MedlinePlus says you may not notice it for weeks to months.

The AAD gives childbirth as an example. A new mother can see heavy shedding about two months after the birth. The shedding usually peaks at about four months.

After COVID-19 the gap can be shorter. In the systematic review, the mean time from the first COVID-19 symptoms to the shedding was 74 days. The authors note that this is earlier than classic telogen effluvium.

So look back, not at last week. Ask what happened two to four months ago. A fever, an operation, a crash diet, a new medicine, or a hard life event are all possible answers.

Recovery is also slow. MedlinePlus says the shedding decreases over 6 to 8 months. The AAD says that within six to nine months the hair tends to regain its normal fullness. If the stress continues, the shedding can last longer.

What else can make hair fall out suddenly?

Stress shedding is common, but it is not the only cause. The AAD describes 18 causes of hair loss. These are the ones that can start quickly:

  • Alopecia areata. The National Institute of Arthritis and Musculoskeletal and Skin Diseases says the immune system attacks the hair follicles. Round or oval patches of hair fall out suddenly. There is usually no rash, redness, or scarring. Hair often regrows.
  • Thyroid disease. The AAD says it can thin the hair, and some people see hair come out in clumps when they brush. Treatment of the thyroid disease can reverse it. Our sister site thyroid.md covers thyroid tests and symptoms.
  • Low nutrient levels. The AAD names too little biotin, iron, protein, or zinc. MedlinePlus also lists anemia.
  • Medicines and cancer treatment. Hair loss is a possible side effect of some medicines. Chemotherapy, or radiation to the head or neck, can cause the loss of most hair within a few weeks.
  • Scalp infection. The AAD says an infection can cause scaly, inflamed areas and hair stubs that look like small black dots. MedlinePlus names ringworm of the scalp.
  • Hormone changes. The AAD names polycystic ovary syndrome as a common cause of hormone imbalance.
  • Scarring alopecia. Inflammation destroys the follicles, so this loss is permanent. Early diagnosis helps prevent more loss.

Too much of a nutrient can also be the problem. The AAD lists large amounts of vitamin A or selenium as a cause of hair loss.

Do not stop a prescribed medicine on your own. The AAD warns that stopping some medicines suddenly can cause serious side effects. Talk to the prescriber first.

What tests will a doctor order for sudden hair loss?

Often, none. MedlinePlus says a careful medical history and an examination of the hair and scalp are usually enough. The clinician will ask about your stress level, recent illness or surgery, diet changes, and hair care.

The AAD describes how a dermatologist finds the cause:

  1. Questions about timing. How long has it gone on, and did it start quickly?
  2. An examination. The dermatologist looks at the scalp, the nails, and any other area with hair loss.
  3. A pull test. A gentle pull on the hair shows how it grows and whether it breaks easily.
  4. A blood test or scalp biopsy. These are used when the dermatologist suspects a disease, a vitamin deficiency, a hormone imbalance, or an infection.

The review above says laboratory tests help to exclude endocrine, nutritional, and autoimmune disorders. MedlinePlus adds that a clinician may look at a plucked hair under a microscope.

Wait for the results before you buy supplements. The AAD advises taking biotin, iron, or zinc only when a blood test shows that you are low. Too much iron can cause poisoning.

When should I see a doctor about hair loss?

MedlinePlus advises a call to your health care provider if you have any of these:

  • Hair loss in an unusual pattern
  • Rapid hair loss, or hair loss at an early age such as your teens or twenties
  • Pain or itching with the hair loss
  • Scalp skin that is red, scaly, or otherwise abnormal
  • Acne, facial hair, or a change in your menstrual cycle
  • Bald spots in your beard or eyebrows
  • Weight gain, muscle weakness, intolerance to cold, or fatigue
  • Areas of infection on the scalp

The last group of symptoms can point to a problem in the whole body, such as a thyroid disorder. Our guides on the causes of fatigue and why you may always feel cold cover those symptoms.

Also book a visit if shedding continues long after the usual recovery period. A systematic review in the American Journal of Clinical Dermatology looked at long-lasting cases labelled chronic telogen effluvium. The authors concluded that many are likely early female pattern hair loss or have a hidden cause. Both need a diagnosis.

Do not wait if the worry itself is heavy. The AAD says that the earlier treatment starts, the more likely hair is to regrow.

The bottom line

Sudden, even shedding is usually telogen effluvium. It starts a few months after an illness, surgery, childbirth, weight loss, or major stress, and it is usually temporary. Fullness tends to return within six to nine months. Patches, a sore or scaly scalp, rapid loss, or symptoms such as fatigue and cold intolerance need a visit. A history and scalp examination find most causes, and blood tests or a biopsy answer the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can stress really make your hair fall out?

Yes. MedlinePlus says physical or emotional stress may cause one half to three quarters of scalp hair to shed, a condition called telogen effluvium. The shedding usually shows weeks to months after the stressful event, not on the same day. It is usually temporary.

Will hair lost from telogen effluvium grow back?

In most cases, yes. The American Academy of Dermatology says the shedding stops as the body readjusts, and hair tends to regain its normal fullness within six to nine months. Shedding can last longer if the stress continues.

Is it normal to lose more hair after having a baby?

Yes. The American Academy of Dermatology says a new mother can see excessive shedding about two months after the birth, with a peak at about four months. It calls this shedding normal and temporary.

Should I take biotin or iron for sudden hair shedding?

Get a blood test first. The American Academy of Dermatology advises taking biotin, iron, or zinc only when a blood test shows that you are low. Too much iron can cause poisoning, and large amounts of vitamin A or selenium can cause hair loss.

How is alopecia areata different from stress shedding?

Stress shedding thins the hair across the whole scalp. Alopecia areata is an autoimmune disease that causes sudden round or oval bald patches, usually with no rash, redness, or scarring. A clinician can tell the two apart with an examination.
