Why Is My Tongue Tingling?

The short answer: A tingling tongue most often comes from something harmless and short-lived, such as a mild reaction to raw fruit, a numbing shot at the dentist, or fast breathing during anxiety. When it lasts for weeks, look for burning mouth syndrome, a low vitamin B12 level, or low calcium. Sudden tingling or numbness with a drooping face, weak arm, or slurred speech can be a stroke. Call 911 right away.

Why would a tongue tingle in the first place?

The tongue has a dense supply of sensory nerves. Anything that irritates those nerves, changes the blood chemistry around them, or sets off an immune reaction can feel like pins and needles. The cause usually shows in the timing.

  • Within minutes of eating: think of a food reaction.
  • After a dental visit: think of the numbing medicine.
  • During a stressful moment: think of fast breathing.
  • Most days for months: think of burning mouth syndrome or a deficiency.
  • All at once with other new symptoms: think of a stroke.

If the tingling is in your hands and feet as well, our guide to numbness and tingling causes covers the wider list. This article stays on the mouth.

Why does my mouth tingle after I eat raw fruit or vegetables?

This is often oral allergy syndrome, also called pollen food allergy syndrome. The American Academy of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology explains that the immune system confuses proteins in certain pollens with proteins in some foods. People with hay fever are the usual group.

The American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology lists these common matches:

  • Birch pollen: apple, almond, carrot, celery, cherry, hazelnut, kiwi, peach, pear, plum.
  • Grass pollen: celery, melons, oranges, peaches, tomato.
  • Ragweed pollen: banana, cucumber, melons, sunflower seeds, zucchini.

The itch or tingle usually fades soon after you swallow or spit out the food. Cooked versions are often fine, because heat changes the protein shape. The same ACAAI page reports that the reaction spread beyond the mouth in nearly 9 percent of patients and reached anaphylactic shock in 1.7 percent. See an allergist if it keeps happening with the same food. Get emergency care for throat tightness, trouble breathing, or hives on the body.

What if my tongue burns or tingles for weeks?

Long-lasting burning, scalding, or tingling with no visible cause fits burning mouth syndrome. The National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research says the tongue is the most common site. The lips and roof of the mouth can also be involved. Some people also notice dry mouth or a metallic taste (see our post on metallic taste in the mouth). NIDCR notes that the pain can last months or years and that it is more common in women, especially after menopause.

NIDCR splits the condition into two types. Primary burning mouth comes from nerve damage. Secondary burning mouth has another cause that a provider can look for, including:

  • Dry mouth from medicines, Sjogren's disease, or radiation.
  • A yeast infection in the mouth.
  • Allergies to dental products or foods.
  • Acid reflux.
  • Diabetes or thyroid problems.
  • Low B vitamins or iron.
  • Anxiety, depression, or stress.

Treating the underlying cause often helps. For primary cases, NIDCR notes that some medicines calm overactive nerves.

Can a vitamin or mineral problem make my tongue tingle?

Yes. Two are worth checking with a simple blood test.

Vitamin B12. MedlinePlus lists a swollen, red tongue among the signs of B12 deficiency anemia. Numbness and tingling in the hands and feet can follow when the deficiency lasts a long time. Risk goes up with a strict vegetarian or vegan diet, pernicious anemia, stomach or bowel surgery, Crohn's disease, heavy alcohol use, and long-term use of acid-reducing medicine.

Low calcium. Calcium helps nerves stay calm. When it drops, nerves fire too easily. MedlinePlus lists tingling lips, fingers, and toes, plus muscle cramps, as the most common symptoms of hypoparathyroidism. The most common cause is injury to the parathyroid glands during thyroid or neck surgery. Tingling around the mouth in the days after that surgery needs a call to your surgeon.

Can anxiety cause a tingling tongue?

It can. During a panic attack, people often breathe fast without noticing. That lowers carbon dioxide in the blood. MedlinePlus lists numbness and tingling in the arms or around the mouth as symptoms of hyperventilation, along with lightheadedness and chest tightness.

MedlinePlus suggests slowing your breath with pursed-lip breathing: pucker your lips and breathe out slowly. Over time, belly breathing, relaxation practice, and regular exercise help. Hyperventilation can also come from medical problems such as infection, bleeding, or a lung clot. So get checked if it is new, or comes with fever, pain, or bleeding. Could a medicine or dental work be the cause?

Dental numbing shots. A lower-jaw injection numbs the lip, chin, and tongue on one side. Tingling as it wears off is expected. In rare cases the needle irritates the lingual nerve, and altered feeling in the tongue lasts longer. A 2023 review in Cureus lists paresthesia among the nerve problems linked to dental anesthesia. Tell your dentist if numbness or tingling is still there the next day.

Blood pressure medicines. ACE inhibitors such as lisinopril can rarely cause swelling of the lips and tongue. MedlinePlus says to contact your provider right away or go to the emergency room if your tongue or lips swell. Early swelling can feel tight or tingly before it shows.

Dry mouth. Many medicines reduce saliva. NIDCR lists dry mouth as a trigger for secondary burning mouth. Bring a full medicine list to your visit.

When is a tingling tongue an emergency?

Call 911 if tingling or numbness starts suddenly with any stroke sign. The CDC lists these signs:

  • Sudden numbness or weakness in the face, arm, or leg, especially on one side.
  • Sudden confusion, trouble speaking, or trouble understanding speech.
  • Sudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes.
  • Sudden trouble walking, dizziness, or loss of balance.
  • Sudden severe headache with no known cause.

Use F.A.S.T.: check for Face drooping, Arm drifting down, and Speech that is slurred. Then it is Time to call 911. The CDC notes that the best stroke treatments work only if the stroke is found within 3 hours of the first symptoms.

Also get emergency care for tongue or throat swelling, trouble breathing or swallowing, or hives after a food, sting, or new medicine.

The bottom line

A brief tingle after raw fruit, a dental visit, or a stressful moment is common and usually fades. Tingling that lasts for weeks deserves a checkup and blood tests for B12, iron, calcium, and blood sugar. A dentist can look for burning mouth syndrome. Sudden tingling with face, arm, or speech changes is a stroke until proven otherwise, so call 911.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a tingling tongue a sign of a stroke?

Usually not, but it can be. A stroke comes on suddenly and often brings face drooping, arm weakness, or slurred speech. If tingling starts with any of those, call 911 right away.

Why does only the tip of my tongue tingle?

Tingling at the tip often follows hot food, acidic fruit, or a raw fruit that triggers oral allergy syndrome. Burning mouth syndrome also often starts on the tip and sides. If it lasts more than a couple of weeks, ask a dentist or doctor to take a look.

How long does tongue numbness last after a dental shot?

Normal numbness wears off within a few hours of the visit, often with a tingly phase at the end. If your tongue is still numb or tingling the next day, call your dentist. Nerve irritation from the injection is rare but should be checked.

What blood tests check for a cause of tongue tingling?

A provider may order a vitamin B12 level, a complete blood count, iron studies, calcium, and blood sugar. Thyroid tests may also be added. These look for deficiencies and conditions linked to burning mouth and nerve symptoms.

Can stress alone make my mouth tingle?

Yes. Anxiety can lead to fast breathing, which lowers carbon dioxide in the blood and causes tingling around the mouth. Slow breathing out through pursed lips often eases it within minutes.
