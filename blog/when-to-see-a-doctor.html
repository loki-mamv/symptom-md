When to See a Doctor: Symptoms You Should Never Ignore

February 9, 2026

Most of us experience minor symptoms on a regular basis: a headache after a long day, a sore throat during cold season, or muscle aches after exercise. These typically resolve on their own and rarely require medical attention. But certain symptoms can signal serious underlying conditions that need prompt evaluation. Knowing the difference can be lifesaving.

Symptoms That Require Emergency Care

The following symptoms should prompt a call to 911 or an immediate visit to the nearest emergency department. Do not wait to see if they resolve on their own.

Chest Pain or Pressure

Any chest pain that is new, severe, or accompanied by shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or pain radiating to the arm, jaw, or back should be treated as a potential heart attack until proven otherwise. This applies regardless of age. While chest pain can have many benign causes (acid reflux, muscle strain, anxiety), the consequences of dismissing a cardiac event are too severe to take chances.

In women, heart attack symptoms are more likely to include shortness of breath, nausea, vomiting, and back or jaw pain rather than the classic crushing chest pain. Do not assume you are too young or too healthy for a cardiac event.

Sudden Severe Headache

A headache that reaches maximum intensity within seconds, often described as "the worst headache of my life," can indicate a subarachnoid hemorrhage (bleeding around the brain). This is a medical emergency with a mortality rate exceeding 30% if untreated. It is different from a migraine, which typically builds over minutes to hours.

Sudden Weakness or Numbness on One Side

Sudden onset of weakness, numbness, or paralysis affecting one side of the body, face, arm, or leg is the hallmark of a stroke. Other stroke symptoms include sudden confusion, difficulty speaking or understanding speech, sudden vision loss, severe dizziness, and loss of coordination. The acronym FAST (Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911) remains the most effective tool for rapid recognition.

Stroke treatment is time-sensitive. Clot-busting medications (tPA) must be administered within 4.5 hours of symptom onset, and every minute of delay results in the death of approximately 1.9 million neurons.

Difficulty Breathing

Sudden onset of severe shortness of breath, particularly if accompanied by chest pain, rapid heart rate, or coughing up blood, can indicate a pulmonary embolism (blood clot in the lung), a severe asthma attack, a collapsed lung, or an allergic reaction (anaphylaxis). Any of these conditions can be life-threatening without prompt treatment.

Symptoms That Warrant a Doctor's Visit Within Days

Unexplained Weight Loss

Losing more than 5% of your body weight over six to twelve months without trying is considered clinically significant unexplained weight loss. In a 40-year-old weighing 160 pounds, that would be 8 pounds or more. Possible causes range from thyroid disorders and diabetes to cancer and depression. A 2017 study in the BMJ found that unexplained weight loss was associated with a cancer diagnosis in about 6% of cases, with the risk increasing with age.

Persistent Fever

A fever that lasts more than three days, recurs frequently, or exceeds 103 degrees Fahrenheit (39.4 degrees Celsius) in an adult warrants evaluation. While most fevers are caused by viral infections and resolve on their own, a persistent or recurring fever can indicate a more serious infection, an autoimmune condition, or, less commonly, cancer.

Blood Where It Should Not Be

Blood in your stool (which can appear bright red or black and tarry), blood in your urine (pink, red, or cola-colored), coughing up blood, or unexplained vaginal bleeding between periods all warrant medical evaluation. These symptoms have many possible causes, most of which are treatable, but some indicate conditions that need prompt attention.

A New or Changing Mole

Any mole that is asymmetric, has irregular borders, is multiple colors, is larger than 6mm (the size of a pencil eraser), or is evolving in size, shape, or color should be evaluated by a dermatologist. These are the ABCDE criteria for melanoma. Caught early, melanoma has a five-year survival rate above 99%. Caught late, it is one of the most lethal cancers.

Symptoms That Can Usually Wait

Not every symptom requires a doctor's visit. The following generally can be managed at home, at least initially:

  • Common cold symptoms (runny nose, mild sore throat, mild cough) lasting less than 10 days
  • Mild headaches that respond to over-the-counter pain medication
  • Minor muscle and joint aches without swelling, redness, or loss of function
  • Brief episodes of dizziness when standing quickly (likely orthostatic hypotension)
  • Mild digestive complaints (bloating, occasional heartburn, mild diarrhea) lasting less than two days

However, any of these symptoms should be evaluated if they persist beyond their expected course, worsen over time, or are accompanied by alarm signs.

A General Rule

When in doubt, err on the side of seeking care. It is always better to have a benign symptom evaluated and be reassured than to ignore a warning sign that turns out to be serious. Your doctor will not judge you for being cautious.
